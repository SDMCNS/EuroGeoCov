📖 Scientific Concepts & Formulas RF Propagation

EuroGeoCov evaluates European airspace line of sight (LoS), radar coverage, and radio navigation aid performance using the 25m EU-DEM dataset combined with rigorous electromagnetic wave propagation models.

1
Earth Curvature & 4/3 Refraction (k = 4/3)
Tropospheric bending of radio waves

Due to atmospheric density decreasing with altitude, radio waves refract (bend) downward toward the Earth. Under standard international atmosphere (ICAO Annex 10 / ITU-R P.834), this is modeled by scaling Earth's physical radius ($R_0 \approx 6,371\text{ km}$) by an effective factor $k = 4/3 \approx 1.3333$:

R' = k × R0 ≈ 8,495 km

The apparent terrain bulge (elevation drop) Δh at distance d1 from transmitter and d2 from receiver is calculated as:

Δh(d) = (d1 × d2) / (2 × R') ≈ (d1 × d2) / 16,990 km

The maximum theoretical radio horizon line-of-sight distance over smooth Earth is:

dhorizon ≈ 4.12 × (√htx + √hrx) km   (h in meters)
dhorizon ≈ 1.23 × (√htx + √hrx) NM   (h in feet)
2
First Fresnel Zone (F₁) & 60% Clearance
Diffraction and multi-path clearance ellipsoid

Radio waves do not travel as a microscopic optical line, but rather in concentric ellipsoidal Fresnel zones where secondary wavelets interfere constructively. The radius of the 1st Fresnel zone at intermediate distances d1, d2 is:

F1 = √[ (λ × d1 × d2) / (d1 + d2) ] = √[ (c × d1 × d2) / (f × (d1 + d2)) ]
Clearance Threshold: If surrounding mountain terrain penetrates inside 60% of the 1st Fresnel zone (0.6 × F1), diffraction loss and phase cancellation begin.
  • Clear LoS: Clearance ≥ 0.6 F1 (free space propagation).
  • Diffracted: Clearance between 0.0 and 0.6 F1 (1 to 6 dB shadow loss).
  • Terrain Blocked: Clearance < 0.0 m (optical ray obstructed by peak).
3
Radar Silence Cones & DME 120° Vertical Slices
Bounded vertical coverage volumes and aperture limits

Ground-based radar dishes (PSR, SSR, Mode-S) and DME/VOR navigation aids transmit within bounded elevation beam windows, leaving dead zones directly overhead (Cone of Silence) and below low-angle cutoffs:

1. Radar Overhead Cone of Silence (∼34° Zenith Angle):
Air traffic surveillance radars typically scan between a low-angle cutoff θmin ≈ 0.5° and an upper elevation cutoff θmax ≈ 34° (corresponding to a 34° zenith cone half-angle directly overhead). Any aircraft flying at elevation angles θ > 34° enters the antenna silence cone.
rcone = (h − hstation) / tan(34°)

At FL300 (h − hsta ≈ 9,144m), the radar blind radius directly overhead is rcone ≈ 13.5 km (7.3 NM).

2. DME 120° Vertical Plane Window (30°–150° Slice):
DME transmissions are bounded in a 120° slice through the vertical plane from the site between 30° and 150°. A beam below 30° elevation angle (θ < 30°) is not covered, and symmetrically above 150° in the vertical plane (below 30° on the opposite side) is not covered. The covered airspace is thus contained in a 120° cone/slice through the vertical plane:
Window: 30° ≤ θvert ≤ 150°  &implies;  Δθ = 120° Vertical Slice
router,beam = (h − hstation) / tan(30°)
3. Bounded Vertical Airspace Annulus:
At any given flight level h, ground-based equipment with both minimum and maximum elevation beam angles serves a strictly bounded horizontal annulus:
rinner = (h − hsta) / tan(θmax)  ≤  d  ≤  router = min[ RDOC, (h − hsta) / tan(θmin) ]
4
Free Space Path Loss (FSPL) & Diffraction
Friis formula & ITU-R P.526 knife-edge attenuation

Free space attenuation for radio signals across slant distance d and frequency f is computed via Friis transmission equation:

FSPL (dB) = 32.44 + 20 log10(dkm) + 20 log10(fMHz)

When terrain penetrates the LoS ray, ITU-R P.526 knife-edge diffraction loss J(v) is calculated using the dimensionless Fresnel-Kirchhoff parameter v:

v = hobst × √[ 2(d1 + d2) / (λ × d1 × d2) ]
J(v) ≈ 6.9 + 20 log10[ √((v − 0.1)² + 1) + v − 0.1 ] dB
5
1,000-ft Airspace Cubes & Multi-Station N-Coverage
Voxelized atmosphere & sensor redundancy

European airspace is voxelized in 1,000-ft vertical increments (Flight Levels FL010 to FL600). The Coverage Floor marks the lowest flight level where line-of-sight and clearance are established above the Alps, Pyrenees, and Carpathians.

Multi-Station Redundancy Tiers:
  • 0 Stations: Radio shadow — blind zone beneath mountain horizon.
  • 1 Station (Single Coverage): Basic RNAV or secondary surveillance tracking.
  • 2 Stations (Dual Redundancy): Fail-safe tracking against single sensor outage.
  • 3+ Stations (WAM Multilateration): High-precision hyperbolic Time Difference of Arrival (TDOA) position verification.
